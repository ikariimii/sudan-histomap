Sources and citation

The dataset has 641 registered sources (data/periods.json → meta.sources). Every era page lists its sources; every map feature opens a card naming its source (and page, where recorded). The offline check tools/verify_citations.py --offline confirms that every cited id resolves in the registry. Rule from the legend spec: no layer without a source.

Borders, points and homelands

An area is drawn only when each feature cites a source, and areas are always approximate (About page). Of the 68 eras, 47 have at least one sourced area and 20 are shown by cited points only. Contested places are drawn as points or dashed lines with amber hatching, never as polygons owned by a state (legend handoff). Border zones (core, claimed, influence, contested) come from the bd-0.1 drafts in db/border_drafts/: anchor points buffered along the Nile or around a centre, with editorial widths, so every zone carries APPROXIMATE; confidence below 0.4 forces EDITORIAL. Confidence words: high ≥0.7, medium ≥0.4, otherwise low. A zone reaches the public map only after the Historian's sign-off gate in that README. Homelands have no dates in the sources read, so they sit on one undated layer, off by default, and are not tied to eras.

CINC versus editorial scores

Era cards can show two different measures of power. Share of regional military-industrial capability (COW CINC): Derived figure: the era's mean share of the combined CINC of the regional states (Sudan, South Sudan, Egypt, Ethiopia, Eritrea, Chad, Libya, CAR) in the years with data. Not comparable with the editorial scores. Source: Correlates of War National Material Capabilities v6.0 (abridged CSV) · link. Editorial score: Editorial ordinal score set by the project from the cited source (0–1), not a measured index.

Population

Each era's population is summarised as a central figure with a low and high value; every estimate behind it carries its own year, territory, method, sources and badges (CENSUS, MODEL, CONTESTED, SINGLE-SOURCE). McEvedy & Jones (1978) was not used because no licensed copy was available, and HYDE 3.3 grids were not aggregated because the download portal could not be reached (db/population.json).

City dating

Cities are drawn by their cited roles in each era. FIRST-ATTESTED means a place is shown from its first mention, although it is older; RUIN-UNDATED means its end date is not cited, so the symbol fades rather than stopping. Dates stay empty where the sources read do not state them (db/cities.json meta).

Translation status

Arabic text that carries the WORKING TRANSLATION flag in the data is marked with * or an "Arabic under review" badge. The review queue (db/ar_review_queue.json) lists 50 eras, 64 artwork captions and 16 social items awaiting native-speaker review; no Arabic text was rewritten by it.

Known gaps

  • Border zones: not every drafted era is signed off, and the README's gate also asks to clip corridors to the Natural Earth river/terrain mask (v0.2); see db/border_drafts/README.md.
  • Not drafted on purpose (no source read supports a limit): Dinka/Nuer/Anuak (homelands layer), Beja, Zande, Wadai, Bornu, Garamantes, Punt, Sabaean, Zagwe, rise of Islam, Ottoman Hejaz, Late Period Egypt, Noba/X-Group, Suakin, Khartoum Neolithic/Mesolithic. War era: no control polygons without a UN/aid-agency citation.
  • Homelands have no dates, so they are not tied to eras.
  • Several eras have no or one city with a cited role (db/city_layers/AUDIT.md).
  • Population: McEvedy & Jones (1978) not used (no licensed copy); HYDE 3.3 grids not aggregated (download portal unavailable to the project).
  • No traffic data exists, so Arabic review is prioritised by an editorial proxy (review queue meta).

Map and image licences

Basemap and display geometry: Natural Earth, public domain (Natural Earth vector data (rivers, disputed areas) · link). Images and flags are used only once their licence is cleared and are credited with author, licence and Commons link in their card (db/README.md, legend handoff). The licences of external atlases we reviewed, and whether we reuse them, are on the Data & sources page.
