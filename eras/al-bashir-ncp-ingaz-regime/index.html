Geopolitics

Bashir took power in the 30 June 1989 coup, which the Arabic sources call the Islamic Movement's coup; the regime called itself al-Ingaz (Salvation). French Wikipedia says it was 'encouraged by the National Islamic Front' of Turabi (AR_WIKI_HIST; FR_WIKI_HIST; RU_WIKI_HIST). Bashir fell out with Turabi in 1999, came to rely on paramilitaries (the RSF, set up in 2011 and placed under the presidency in 2017), and was the only sitting head of state indicted by the ICC (ICG2019). The 2005 CPA ("Naivasha" in Arabic) split oil revenue and led to South Sudan's secession after a referendum with c.99% for independence (ES_ELCANO2011; RU_WIKI_HIST). Arabic sources blame the secession on the regime's insistence on a religious state (AR_SUDANILE2013). Darfur from 2003: German sources frame it as centre–periphery conflict, Chinese scholarship as a tribal resource conflict that 'cannot be blamed on China' (DE_BPB_KONFLIKT; ZH_YU2008; ZH_SUN2009). Ended by the April 2019 coup (ICG2019).

Culture

Islamisation from the early 1990s (RU_WIKI_HIST); sharia applied in the North only under the CPA (FR_WIKI_HIST). Sources disagree on naming: 'Islamic Movement coup'/Ingaz (ar), 'Islamic Republic' (fr, not an official name), 'al-Bashir regime / National Islamic Front' (ru).

Economy

Oil: with CNPC's help Sudan went from oil importer to exporter in 1999 (ZH_YU2008); investment came from China, Malaysia and India (DE_WIKI_HIST). RIA (2011): reserves of 6bn barrels and 510,000 bpd, mostly in the South, exported by pipeline across the North. Opposing readings: an Arabic opinion source says oil revenue never reached production and debt rose to USD 43bn (AR_SUDANILE2013), while German Wikipedia says militias carried out 'ethnic cleansing' to secure the oilfields. ICG: patronage spending on the security sector took by some estimates 70% of the budget, and the 2011 loss of southern oil deepened the slump that sparked the 2018–19 revolution.
