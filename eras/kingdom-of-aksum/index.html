Capitals / sites

Aksum, Adulis

Geopolitics

Ptolemy knew both Meroe and Adulis; Aksumite envoys visited Meroe as friends, and while Zoscales ruled the Eritrean coast, Meroe still held the Beja desert (GHA II ch.15, p.381). Aksum conquered the declining Meroitic kingdom in the 4th century. The empire then covered northern Ethiopia, parts of the Sudan and southern Arabia, with vassal kings including those of Himyar and 'Alwa (p.384). Ezana's titles set him apart from the Kasu and Beja (ch.16, p.411), and his DAE 11 inscription records a victory over the Noba beyond the Takazze (p.416).

Culture

Kobishanov sees Meroitic influence predominating at Aksum in the 2nd–3rd centuries (GHA II ch.15, p.399), including in architecture (p.395). Followers of the Egyptian-Meroitic religion lived at Aksum, Adulis and Matara (p.397). Aksum also issued Greek-legend coins and wrote Ge'ez (p.398).

Economy

The Periplus names Koloè as the ivory market and Adulis as the market town (GHA II ch.14, p.369). Meroitic objects found at Aksum were trade goods or, more likely, spoils of war or tribute. Cotton cloth and iron may have come from Meroe, and gold from Sasu and perhaps from Beja country (ch.15, p.389).
