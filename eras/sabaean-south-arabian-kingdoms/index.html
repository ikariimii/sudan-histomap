Geopolitics

GHA II gives no direct link between Saba and the Sudan; Saba reaches this story through the Horn. Kings of D'mt styled themselves 'mukarrib of D'iamat and Saba'' (GHA II ch.13, pp.353–354). Later the flow reversed: Sabaean inscriptions of the 2nd–3rd centuries record Aksumites fighting in Yemen (ch.15, p.381), and Ezana called himself king of Himyar and Saba (ch.16, p.411).

Culture

In the Ethiopian highlands, the South Arabian period is marked by the Yeha temple, its links to Marib, and the Sabaean gods Almaqah and 'Astar (GHA II ch.13, pp.343, 345, 352). Local culture absorbed these into an 'Ethiopo-Sabaean' synthesis (p.355).

Economy

South Arabia supplied incense to Egypt before Egyptian ships went to Punt for it directly (GHA II ch.4, p.145). No Sabaean trade with the Sudan is documented in GHA II.
