Alodia (Alwa)

Capitals / sites

Soba

Geopolitics

Southernmost of the three Christian Nubian kingdoms by the end of the 6th c. (Nobadia, Makuria, Alodia) (GHA II ch.12).

Culture

A Byzantine mission under Bishop Longinos reached Alodia c.580 with Nobadian support and found it already partly converted by the Aksumites (GHA II ch.12).

Economy

Kropáček says the usual picture of a flourishing Alodia rests mainly on Ibn Sulaym (from 975) and Abu Salih (early 13th c.), plus Muslim merchants' reports, 'for 'Alwa was a good market for the purchase of slaves'. Abu Salih describes the kingdom 'in full prosperity, with some 400 churches'. In its decline, Arab immigrants took control of pastures, and its human resources were 'probably already weakened by the slave trade' (GHA IV ch.16, p.406). No account of production or taxation was read. GHA III adds Ibn Salim al-Aswani's account, quoted by al-Maqrizi: Soba had magnificent buildings and gardens and 'churches overflowing with gold', and Alodia's king ruled 'a land that was far more fertile' than Makuria. Jakobielski stresses 'we know next to nothing of Alodia' (GHA III ch.8, p.196). Ibn Hawqal reports that pastoral peoples in Kordofan owed allegiance to the king of Makuria or of Alodia (p.199).
