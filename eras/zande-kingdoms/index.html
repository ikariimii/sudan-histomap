Geopolitics

In the 18th century the Vungara clan became sole chiefs of the Mbomu and began a long eastward and southward expansion, lasting until the late 1800s, that conquered or absorbed many peoples (GHA V ch.18, p.523). By 1800 the Zande conquest was beginning south of Dar Fertit, where Darfur slave-raiders were active (ch.7, p.199). The Zande state's strength helped it resist the Khartoum slavers; in 1870 chief Ndoruma defeated a 2,000-strong force and killed the trader Abu Qurun (GHA VI ch.14, p.368). Yambio fought both the Condominium and the Belgians; his patrol attacks in 1903–4 and a 10,000-strong attack on a Belgian post weakened Zande power (GHA VII ch.4, p.81).

Culture

M'Bokolo describes Zande society as 'an extraordinary puzzle of uprooted clans and lineages' bound by common allegiance to the ruling Vungara aristocracy. The conquering elites prized hunting, learned farming from the conquered, and left field work to women, clients and slaves (GHA V ch.18, p.523). Under Avungara rule, 'Zande' came to mean subject-farmer (GHA VI ch.12, p.300).

Economy

The Zande developed high-yield farming that fed a dense population, about 40 per km² in the 1870s according to Schweinfurth, swelled by captives taken in the aristocracy's raids (GHA VI ch.12, p.300). In the 19th century, Sudanese traders built zaribas and fortified bases in Zande country and mixed state monopoly and private trade with armed force (p.306). Before 1800, Darfur took copper tribute near Hofrat en-Nahas, north of Zande lands (GHA V ch.7, p.199).
