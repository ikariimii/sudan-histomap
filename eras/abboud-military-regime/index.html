Khartoum

Geopolitics

Ended in 1964 by a civilian-led popular uprising, which GHA VIII calls exceptional in Africa ('political succession after a popular uprising').

Culture

In the south the regime 'suppressed expressions of religious and cultural differences and bolstered attempts to arabize society'; in Feb 1964 Abboud ordered 'the mass expulsion of foreign missionaries from the south' (LOC1991_ABBUD, 1991). A UNESCO MGIEP case study says mission schools were brought under government control, Arabic was progressively introduced as the medium of instruction, and missionaries were restricted until all were expelled in 1964 (UNESCO_MGIEP_SSD, n.d.) [VERIFY]. Seri-Hersch traces these Arabisation policies back to the late colonial years (Arabic declared the vehicular language in 1949; Arabic-medium government schools in the south by 1954) and says the post-1964 parliamentary governments did not significantly change them (SERIHERSCH2020, 2020, §§9–10) [VERIFY]. In the north, the Ansar and Khatmiyya lost influence under military rule, and Abd al-Rahman al-Mahdi died in early 1959 (LOC1991_ABBUD).

Economy

The Gezira scheme, 'the backbone of the Sudan economy', had about 26,000 tenant farmers in 1958, the year of the coup (GHA VIII p.322). No source read covers economic policy under Abboud.
