  • Elephantine (find-spot, not a border) [low] — G. Mokhtar (ed.), UNESCO International Scientific Committee (1981) General History of Africa, II: Ancient Civilizations of Africa pp. 280

Dating note

Reign dates follow UNESCO GHA II ch.10 (Leclant, 1981); later scholarship revises several (incl. the Shabaka/Shebitku order) – treat as approximate. WHC gives Napatan 900–270 BCE; -800 start kept as display convention. Coordinates: Pleiades/Wikidata.

Key events

  • Alara, then Kashta (c.760–750 BCE); Kushites in Upper Egypt c.750
  • Peye (Piye/'Piankhi') reign from c.751 BCE; Victory Stela; conquest of Egypt c.728/727
  • Shabaka from c.713 BCE founds 25th Dynasty rule over all Egypt
  • Shabataka (Shebitku) c.700–690; Taharqa 690–664 BCE (cenotaph/pyramid at Nuri)
  • Tanwetamani (Tanoutamon) 664–c.656; Assyrian sack of Thebes 663; Kushite withdrawal
  • Atlanarsa 653–643, Senkamanisken 643–623, Anlamani 623–593, Aspelta 593–568 BCE
  • Psamtik II's expedition captures Napata (GHA: 591 BCE; widely dated 593 BCE)
  • Royal burials at El-Kurru then Nuri; burial shift to Meroe c.270 BCE

Capitals / sites

Jebel Barkal / Napata, El-Kurru, Nuri, Kawa (Gematon)

Geopolitics

Kushite kings ruled Egypt as the 25th Dynasty; Shabaka 'brought the entire Nile valley as far as the Delta under the empire of Kush' (GHA II). Assyria sacked Thebes 663 BCE and the Kushites withdrew; Psamtik II captured Napata (591/593 BCE).

Culture

Strong Egyptian religious and funerary influence (Amun cult at Jebel Barkal, pyramids at El-Kurru and Nuri).

Economy

Agriculture and herding developed; gold mining an important activity (PRC Embassy text, popular source).
