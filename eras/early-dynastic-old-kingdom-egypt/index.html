Geopolitics

Egypt reached the Second Cataract at Buhen by the reign of Djer (1st Dynasty) (GHA II ch.8, p.234). Snefru's annals on the Palermo Stone record campaigns against the Nubians (ch.2, p.92). Adam gives the figures as 110,000 prisoners and 200,000 cattle from Ta-Seti (ch.8, p.235), while Zayed cites 7,000 men (ch.4, p.141 fn.14). In the 6th Dynasty, Harkhuf of Elephantine made four journeys to Yam and found Irtet, Setu and Wawat 'united under one chief' (ch.9, p.251).

Culture

From the Old Kingdom, Egypt saw the Sudan as a source of manpower and livestock. 'Ta-Seti', its name for Lower Nubia, means 'land of the bow' (GHA II ch.4, p.141 and fn.14). An Old Kingdom settlement at Buhen has Pharaonic seals from the late 4th and 5th Dynasties (ch.8, p.235). Harkhuf brought a dancing dwarf from Yam for the young Pepi II (ch.9, p.251).

Economy

From about 2500 BC the Egyptians set up furnaces at Buhen to smelt local copper, which proves 'African copper was produced at this time' (GHA II ch.4, p.142 fn.15; ch.8, p.235). Harkhuf returned from Yam 'with 300 donkeys loaded with incense, ebony, oil, panther skins, elephant tusks' and other goods (ch.9, p.251).
