Geopolitics

Before independence, Libya touched Sudan through the Sanusi order: its centre at Jaghbub lay on the main trade route 'between the Sudan and the coast', and in 1895 it moved its headquarters south to Kufra (LOC_LBY1987_SANUSI). Kufra, the last Sanusi stronghold, fell to Italy in 1931 (LOC_LBY1987_ITALOSANUSI), and a 1934 accord with Britain and Egypt transferred the Sarra Triangle, a corner of the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan, to Italian control (LOC_LBY1987_FOURTHSHORE). Under Qaddafi, relations swung between hostility and cordiality: Nimeiri hosted anti-Qaddafi broadcasts while Libya trained anti-Nimeiri forces and supported the SPLM; Sadiq al-Mahdi let Libyan forces base in Darfur to help Chadian rebels until Sudan asked them to leave in 1988 under Egyptian and US pressure; after 1989 a unity agreement was negotiated (1990) and Qaddafi visited Khartoum (LOC1991_LIBYA). [REPORTED – UN PoE, interview-based] UN Panel of Experts reports describe a southern Libya supply line for the RSF. In 2023, fuel and Land Cruisers came from southern Libya, and one car convoy passed through Kufra. Libyan sources said the Kufra-based Subul al-Salam brigade and the 128 Brigade helped resupply the RSF (UNPOE_S2024_65, paras. 44–47) [SINGLE-SOURCE – S/2024/65 para. 47]. In 2024, the RSF sought to control smuggling routes from Kufra to El Malha and Mellit, and from Sabha to Zuruk. Supplies from southern Libya and eastern Chad were routed through a hub at Bir Mirgui, and an RSF ammunition convoy from southern Libya was intercepted in North Darfur in November 2024 (UNPOE_S2025_239, paras. 71–72, 98, 104).

Economy

In 1990 Libya and Sudan signed a trade and development protocol providing Libyan investment in Sudanese agriculture in exchange for guaranteed access to Sudanese food, and agreed to plan easier travel between Darfur and Libya's Al Khalij area (LOC1991_LIBYA) [SINGLE-SOURCE].
