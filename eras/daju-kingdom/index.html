Daju kingdom

Jebel Marra region

Geopolitics

Balfour-Paul (as summarised by Theobald 1965, reported by Takana 2016, p.51) says the Dajo sultanate controlled the southern part of Darfur, while the Tunjur held the north. Its founders were immigrants from abroad, and power passed between the Dajo, Tunjur and Fur 'without much bloodshed'. Arkell went further and called the Dajo and Tunjur kingdoms extensions of Borno (Takana 2016, p.6). That claim is contested and is reported here only second-hand. Dates and limits are not given in these sources.

Culture

Dajo oral traditions claim a migration into Darfur from the east (the Nile), although the Dajo also have clans around Lake Chad (Takana 2016, p.68). Takana says all the Darfur kingdoms, including the Dajo, 'were associated with the Arabs from the beginning' (Takana 2016, p.52). He dates Darfur's customary law to 'the rise of the early Darfur sultanates of Dajo and Tunjur' (p.109). Oral tradition, reported second-hand.

Economy

Hasan & Ogot report the oral tradition that the Daju kingdom 'flourished during the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries' and that, 'on losing control over the trade', the Daju were succeeded by the Tunjur early in the 15th century (GHA V ch.7, p.187). Which trade is not specified. Oral tradition, reported second-hand.
