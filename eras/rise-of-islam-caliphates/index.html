Geopolitics

Early Muslim converts fled to Christian Ethiopia and were received by the Negus (GHA III ch.2, p.35). In 651–2 an Arab army reached Dongola; Nubian resistance led to the bakt, which El Fasi and Hrbek call a non-aggression pact. It lasted some 600 years and kept Nubia as a sphere of influence rather than a province (ch.3, pp.81–82). Partial treaties were made with the Beja (p.82), and in 1315 a Muslim king on the Dongola throne ended the bakt (p.83).

Culture

From the 8th century, Arab nomads moved into the lands between the Nile and the Red Sea, and Arabs intermarried with Beja chiefs (GHA III ch.3, p.82). Most Beja were formally Islamized by the 14th century (p.83).

Economy

Mecca was a commercial centre on the caravan route, and the pilgrimage brought profit (GHA III ch.2, p.33). The bakt allowed free travel and trade (ch.3, p.81). Arab merchants had a quarter in Dongola, and from the 8th century they settled in 'Aydhab, Badi' and Sawakin (p.82).
