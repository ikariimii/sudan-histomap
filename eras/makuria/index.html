Dating note

End date approximate (kingdom fragmented in 14th–15th c.). Merger with Makuria: date disputed. GHA II ch.12 (Michałowski, pp.332–333) gives two versions: Maqrizi has one king, Qalidurut, ruling central and northern Nubia by the mid-7th c.; 'Christian sources seem to prove that the union of Nubia was the work of King Merkurios, who came to the throne in 697.' Derda & Łajtar (2019, p.136 and n.4): 'perhaps around 630'; the earliest indubitable proof of a greater Makuria including Nobadia is the reign of Merkurios (late 7th–early 8th c.); incorporation probably before the Arab raid on Dongola in 652; Godlewski has suggested the end of the 6th c. Welsby 2002 not read on this point.

Key events

  • Repulsed Arab armies at Dongola 651/652
  • Baqt treaty with Egypt
  • Faras cathedral frescoes
  • Decline under Mamluk pressure, 14th c.

Capitals / sites

Old Dongola, Banganarti

Geopolitics

Kingdom centred on Old Dongola from the 6th c.; the baqt agreement with Muslim Egypt, followed by Abdallah ibn Abi Sarh's raid on Dongola in 651–2; Northern and Central Nubia later united into one state (GHA II ch.12).

Culture

Converted to Melkite (Orthodox) Christianity by Justinian's mission (567–70), then adopted Monophysitism between 660 and 700 (King Merkurios, from 697, is credited with introducing it); government nomenclature modelled on Byzantine bureaucracy; Polish excavations at Old Dongola identified churches and the royal palace (GHA II ch.12).

Economy

Raids did not interrupt the constant trade between Nubia and Muslim Egypt (GHA II ch.12).
