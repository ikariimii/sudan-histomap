Origins & Kerma · Sudan – Middle Nile

Egyptian New Kingdom rule in Nubia

1500 BCE – 1070 BCE

Approximate area of control No data

Dating note

WHC: Gebel Barkal sacred to Amun since c.1500 BCE. Soleb/Kurgus coordinates from Wikidata (approximate).

Key events

  • Viceroy of Kush administers Nubia
  • Temples at Jebel Barkal, Soleb, Abu Simbel

Capitals / sites

Jebel Barkal / Napata, Soleb, Kurgus

Geopolitics

Ruled by a viceroy, the 'King's son of Kush', directly responsible to Pharaoh; Nubia was split into Wawat (Nekhen to the Second Cataract) and Kush (Second to Fourth Cataract). Native chiefs kept sovereignty in their districts (GHA II ch.9, p.270).

Culture

Egyptianisation: after early resistance, temples were built all over Nubia by the kings of the 18th and 19th dynasties; the viceroy was also the religious head of the country (GHA II ch.9, p.270).

Economy

Gold was 'the principal product of Nubia', mined around Wadi el-Allaqi, Wadi Gabgaba and along the Nile to Abu Hamed; Wawat's annual tribute under Thutmose III was 550 pounds. Tribute also included ebony, ivory, incense, cattle, skins and, by the late 18th Dynasty, manufactured goods (GHA II ch.9, pp.272–273).
