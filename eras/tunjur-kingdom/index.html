Geopolitics

Balfour-Paul (via Theobald 1965, reported by Takana 2016, p.51) says the Tunjur sultanate controlled northern Darfur, with the Dajo in the south. It was founded by immigrants, it had a strong central administration, and it gave way to the Fur 'without much bloodshed'. The last Tunjur sultan, 'from whom the power was transferred to Fur Keira', is said to have had Arab lineage (Takana 2016, p.52). Arkell's view that the Tunjur kingdom was an extension of Borno is contested (Takana 2016, p.6).

Culture

'Islam was known as a religion in the Tunjur sultanate era', but it became dominant only under the Fur sultan Sulayman Solonga (Balfour-Paul via Takana 2016, p.51). Takana says 'the Tunjur sultans first introduced Islam into the area'. He calls the founders' lineage and tribal culture 'Arab in most respects', citing a lineage document that mentions the last sultan's property in Medina (pp.51–52). Tunjur oral traditions claim an eastern (Nile) origin, with clans also around Lake Chad (p.68).

Economy

Under the Tunjur (c.1400–1600), Islamic influences 'resulted from wide commercial contact with the Muslim world' and from Arab immigration (GHA V ch.7, p.187). Wadai paid tribute to the Tunjur kings and later continued paying it to the Keira sultanate (p.188).
