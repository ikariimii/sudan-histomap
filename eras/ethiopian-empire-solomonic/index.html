Geopolitics

The Solomonids sent slaves, gold and gifts to the Mamluk sultans whenever they needed a new Egyptian bishop, but grew more aggressive after Amde-Siyon (1314–44). Al-Maqrizi reports that Dawit (1380–1412) invaded the Aswan region (GHA IV ch.17, p.450). Ethiopia fought two border wars with the Funj: one in 1618–19, which Ethiopia won, and one in 1744, when Iyasu II was routed on the Dindera (GHA V ch.7, p.178). Yohannes IV demanded Matamma and Bogos back from Egypt (GHA VI ch.15, p.409), agreed in 1884 to help evacuate Egyptian garrisons from the Sudan, and was killed fighting the Mahdists at Matamma on 10 March 1889 (GHA VII ch.11, pp.267–268).

Culture

The Church stayed spiritually dependent on Alexandria and was led by Egyptian bishops, though a late-15th-century movement tried, and failed, to break away (GHA IV ch.17, pp.445–446). To the Christians of Ethiopia, Sennar was the main land route to the outside world; new bishops from Egypt and European missionaries came that way (GHA V ch.7, p.178).

Economy

Amde-Siyon's conquests gave the kingdom control over the inland end of the Gulf of Aden trade routes (GHA IV ch.17, p.434). Funj–Ethiopian relations show 'positive co-operation and economic interdependence' (GHA V ch.7, p.178). Ethiopian goods reached Sennar, and later Shendi, which Burckhardt called the market for Ethiopian and Fur commerce (p.183). Ethiopians were among the slaves sold at Shendi (p.184).
