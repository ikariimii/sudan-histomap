Khartoum

Geopolitics

Muhammad 'Ali conquered the disintegrating Funj lands in 1820–1. Ibrahim (GHA VI) calls this the 'first colonial era', ruled by a Turkish-speaking elite in the name of the Ottoman sultan. Egyptian claims of sovereignty by conquest (Shukri's 'theory of the vacuum') are disputed. Resistance included the 1822 revolt (Makk Nimr burned Isma'il at Shendi), the Kassala mutiny of 1865 and Fur risings (Amir Harun 1877–80).

Culture

Rulers known to Sudanese as 'Turks'; government favour to the Shayqiyya and the Khatmiyya order bred rivalries (GHA VI ch.14; GHA VII ch.4).

Economy

Primary aim, per archival studies cited by Ibrahim: Sudanese manpower (slave raids, ghazwas, for the army) and gold, sought around Fazughli without success. Agriculture improved by Egyptian experts. A state monopoly exported indigo, gum, ivory and cattle to Egypt. Heavy cash taxes caused flight and revolt. White Nile slave traffic grew from 1840, with zeribas (forts) in the South (GHA VI ch.14).
