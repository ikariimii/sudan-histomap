Geopolitics

Eritrea's war of independence (1961, about 30 years) sent hundreds of thousands of refugees into eastern Sudan; camps there held over a million people at their late-1980s peak, and the 1998 Ethiopia–Eritrea war brought a new influx (SAS_HSBA_WP3). Sudan openly backed Eritrean liberation movements from the late 1970s (LOC_ETH1991_BORDER). After independence, Eritrea hosted the Sudanese opposition: Beja Congress politicians moved there in the early 1990s to join the NDA; the Rashaida Free Lions (1999) had 'strong support' from Asmara; in 2005 they formed the Eastern Front, whose dependence on Eritrea weakened it. Eritrea mediated the Eastern Sudan Peace Agreement of 14 Oct 2006, which reopened the border and improved Asmara–Khartoum ties (SAS_HSBA_WP3). From 1996 the SPLA also based a 'New Sudan Brigade' in western Eritrea (HRW1998_SPLA) [VERIFY]. In the Tigray war period Eritrea kept influence among cross-border tribes such as the Beni Amer (GEO-015). In the 2023 war Eritrea backs the army (ICG_B204) and has aided army-aligned militias in the east (ICG_ETH_ERI); Sudan War Monitor reports two Eritrea-backed militias, the UPFLJ 'Eastern Corps' and NMJD, deploying in Kassala in late Oct 2024 (SWM_ERI2024) [VERIFY]. Colonial phase (Italian colony from 1890) not covered by a read source [thin; WP:Eritrea only].

Economy

The 2006 ESPA provided a four-year USD 600 million development fund for the eastern region, controlled in practice by the NCP (SAS_HSBA_WP3) [SINGLE-SOURCE]. The east is described as an economic frontier with road, rail and oil-pipeline links and cross-border smuggling to Eritrea and Egypt (SAS_HSBA_WP3).
