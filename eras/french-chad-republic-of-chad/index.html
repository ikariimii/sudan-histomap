Geopolitics

Chad's conflicts spill directly into Darfur. In the 1980s western Darfur was a battleground between Hissène Habré's troops and Libyan-backed rebels under Idriss Déby, a Zaghawa supported by Darfur's Zaghawa; in May 1990 Chadian soldiers entered al-Fashir; Déby's MPS armed Zaghawa and Arab militias who turned on the Fur; after Déby took power in Dec 1990 he closed the SPLM office (LOC1991_CHAD). After years of proxy war, the 15 Jan 2010 N'Djamena Agreement committed each side to end support for the other's rebels by 21 Feb 2010 and created a frontier-security protocol (NDJAMENA2010) [VERIFY]; a 3,000-strong joint border force deployed under joint command at El Geneina, then Abéché; Déby visited Khartoum on 8–9 Feb 2010, the first such visit since 2004, and helped negotiate the Feb 2010 Doha framework with JEM (UNSC_S2010_217) [VERIFY]. In the 2023 war Chad is described as aligned more closely with the RSF and as having allegedly let the UAE ship arms into Sudan from its territory (ICG_B204) [VERIFY; allegation], while hosting a major refugee influx: Chad prepared for at least 120,000 more Sudanese after the RSF took El Fasher (GEO-079); see also GEO-037, GEO-038.
