Geopolitics

Resistance to the early Condominium was local in character. It was led by the Nuer near the Sobat and White Nile, culminating in the 1927 revolt, and by the Azande under Yambio, who was defeated in 1905 and died in prison (GHA VII ch.4, pp.80–81). British policy developed the South 'in strict separation from the north': proselytising Islam was forbidden, Christian missions were encouraged, and mission schools taught in English. 'Two totally separate and mutually alien regions' resulted. In August 1955 the Equatoria Corps revolted against the northern takeover of posts (GHA VIII, pp.149–150).

Culture

Peoples of the South are not generally Muslims and do not claim Arab descent; islamisation and arabisation 'were restrained during the Condominium period, when European and American missionaries effected a limited christianization of the region' (HD2011, intro pp.2–3).
