Geopolitics

Ethiopia–Sudan relations ran through mutual proxy war. LOC says relations were 'generally good until the mid-1980s'; Haile Selassie had mediated the 1972 settlement, but Ethiopia resented Eritrean guerrillas operating from Sudan; Sudan recalled its ambassador in Jan 1977, openly backed Eritrean movements, a 1980 treaty eased tensions, and the 1981 Ethiopia–Libya–South Yemen pact and Ethiopian support for the SPLA revived them (LOC_ETH1991_BORDER). HRW says the Derg gave the SPLA uniforms, logistics, arms, training 'and even political direction', and that when Mengistu fell in 1991 the SPLA and several hundred thousand refugees fled back into Sudan (HRW1998_SPLA) [VERIFY] [SINGLE-SOURCE]; an archive-based study adds supply routes, a radio station and Gambella camps (EJOSSAH_DERG_SPLA) [VERIFY]. Under the federal government (since 1991) the flashpoints are the GERD, built 30 km from the border, and al-Fashaga: Sudan's claim rests on the 1902 Anglo-Ethiopian Treaty and the 1903 Gwynn Line; Ethiopian farmers cultivated it from 1996 with Khartoum's tacit acquiescence, until the SAF retook almost all of it after the Tigray war began in Nov 2020 (GEO-015) [CONTESTED] [SINGLE-SOURCE]. In the 2023 war, Ethiopia is described as leaning towards the RSF (ICG_ETH_ERI; ICG_B204) [VERIFY]; GEO-056 (single anonymous Sudanese government source) alleges training and supply [SINGLE-SOURCE, contested].

Culture

Along Sudan's border the culture of western Ethiopia shades into Sudan's. The western lowland strip between the plateau and the Sudanese border 'belongs politically to Ethiopia but whose inhabitants are related to the people of Sudan' (LOC_ETH1991_TOPO). Nilo-Saharan speakers – East Sudanic (Nuer, Anuak), Koman and Berta – live 'in the southwest and west along the border with Sudan', and were in the past 'often the object of slave raids by their neighbors in Ethiopia and Sudan' (LOC_ETH1991_NILOSAHARAN). In Islam, Ethiopian Muslims, 'as in neighboring Sudan and Somalia', are associated with Sufi orders (LOC_ETH1991_ISLAM). [Dated 1991; nothing read on the federal period after 1991]

Economy

Chatham House reports that the SAF's 2020 move gave Sudan control over '600,000 acres of valuable agricultural land' in al-Fashaga, with sesame and other cash crops previously exported via Ethiopia; GERD water releases have disrupted Sudan's Roseires dam (unannounced release 2020; silt clogging turbines 2021), and in mid-2022 the parties agreed to share GERD operating data; Bashir had expected flood control, irrigation and cheap surplus electricity from the dam (GEO-015) [SINGLE-SOURCE for the acreage]. See GEO-016/GEO-017 for the energy-trade argument.
