Geopolitics

Under the 12th Dynasty, Nubia was 'effectively occupied as far as Semna', where Senusret III set up a stela as 'an unmistakable boundary mark' forbidding Nubians to pass downstream (GHA II ch.9, p.255). Senusret III reduced Lower Nubia to 'the status of a province of Egypt' (ch.2, p.98). The kings built fourteen forts in Nubia 'to safeguard their frontiers and trading expeditions' (ch.4, p.143). After the Middle Kingdom collapsed, an independent kingdom of Kush held Nubia south of Elephantine, and the Hyksos king Apophis sought its alliance (ch.9, p.261).

Culture

Stelae from Buhen show Egyptian families living in Nubia for long periods in the Middle Kingdom. They had Egyptian names and 'worshipped the local gods' (GHA II ch.4, p.143). Abu Bakr suggests Amenemhet I may have founded the fortified trading post at Kerma, 'seemingly a centre of Egyptian influence from the reign of Senusret I' (ch.2, p.98). Sherif notes that this reading of Kerma's Western Deffufa has been questioned (ch.9, p.259).

Economy

Rocks in the Second Cataract were circumvented by a slipway for hauling boats, and a dam was built at Semna to ease navigation (GHA II ch.8, p.239). Adam argues that the Theban pharaohs knew Africa south of Semna was 'richly endowed with the raw materials and manpower Egypt lacked' (p.241). Inscriptions of Senusret I's reign record expeditions to Punt by sea (ch.4, p.145).
