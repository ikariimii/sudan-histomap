Thebes

Geopolitics

Kush ended when Tuthmose I 'conquered Nubia beyond the Fourth Cataract' (GHA II ch.9, p.261). A viceroy, titled 'King's son of Kush' from the reign of Tuthmose IV, governed Wawat (Nekhen to the Second Cataract) and Kush (Second to Fourth Cataracts). He commanded garrisons and the troops escorting expeditions to the gold mines, and he delivered Nubia's tribute to the vizier at Thebes (p.270). Under Ramses XI the viceroy Pa-nehesi and his Nubian troops intervened in Upper Egypt, and Herihor later became viceroy, vizier and then king (p.274).

Culture

Egyptian administration brought 'complete egyptianization of Nubia': Nubians adopted Egyptian religion, burial rites and techniques. Princes' sons were educated at the royal court, where they 'forgot their own language'; Djehuty-hotep, Prince of Serra, lived as an upper-class Egyptian (GHA II ch.9, p.271). Yoyotte notes that Egypt 'firmly implanted the worship of the Theban god [Amun] in the land of Kush' (ch.3, p.128). Zayed adds that, although egyptianized, the people of the upper valley 'did not become Egyptian' (ch.4, p.144).

Economy

'The principal product of Nubia was gold.' Under Tuthmose III the annual tribute of Wawat alone was 550 pounds. Gold came from the Wadi Allaqi and Wadi Gabgaba mines in the eastern desert and from mines along the Nile as far south as Abu Hamed. Other tribute included ebony, ivory, cattle, and by the late 18th Dynasty manufactured goods such as shields, stools and beds (GHA II ch.9, p.273). Seti I and Ramses II tried to supply the gold-mine route with water (p.269).
