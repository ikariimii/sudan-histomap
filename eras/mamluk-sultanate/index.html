Geopolitics

Baybars (1260–77) began active intervention in Nubia, motivated largely by Egypt's security, though Hasan also suggests an economic motive (GHA IV ch.16, p.402). King Dawud raided Egypt and took 'Aydhab in 1272. In 1276 a Mamluk expedition defeated him and enthroned his rival Shakanda, whose oath 'would practically have replaced the traditional bakt relationship by complete vassalage' (pp.402–403). King Shamamun resisted, then in 1290 offered an augmented baqt to Qalawun; further expeditions followed from 1305 (p.403). Nubia was never annexed (p.405).

Culture

Mamluk punitive campaigns against Bedouin in Upper Egypt (1302, 1351, 1353, 1378, 1395) left them 'only one escape route ... into the Sudan', and nomads moved in growing numbers towards riverain Nubia (GHA IV ch.16, p.402). Under the 1276 terms, Nubians who did not embrace Islam paid jizya (p.403), and those who converted were protected against enslavement (p.416).

Economy

Shakanda promised an annual tribute of half the country's revenue plus a set number of animals, and the revenue of Maris went to the sultan (GHA IV ch.16, p.403). Sources put the captives taken to Egypt at 10,000, a figure Kropáček calls exaggerated (p.403). Ibn al-Furat estimated that 40,000 tribesmen and camp followers joined the 1289 campaign in search of booty (p.403).
