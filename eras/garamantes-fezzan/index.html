Capitals / sites

Garama (Germa)

Geopolitics

Salama calls the Garamantes, with Garama (Jerma) as chief town, the only organized state of the Saharan interior. They dominated the Fezzan and took tribute from its peoples. They resisted Rome, were beaten in −19 and +69, and then became a client state; their civilization lasted about ten centuries, up to the Arabs (GHA II ch.20, p.522). Roman sources have Maternus travelling with the Garamantian king against the 'Ethiopians' as far as Agisymba (p.517); the location is uncertain.

Culture

The Garamantian state had a Berber-type tribal hierarchy under a supreme chief (agueklid). The central and southern Sahara stayed animist; a claimed late-6th-century conversion is not confirmed by archaeology (GHA II ch.20, p.522). Their famous chariots were probably parade vehicles, not war or trade carts (p.528).

Economy

The Garamantes channelled Saharan trade north to the Syrtic ports. From Garama, routes ran east towards the Nile valley, either by the northern oases (Zuila, Zella, Aujila, Siwa) or by a southern line through Kufra (GHA II ch.20, p.526). This is the only Sudan-facing link in the source, and it concerns routes, not recorded exchange.
