Mecca/Riyadh

Geopolitics

After 1517 the Ottoman sultans, like the Mamluks before them, became guardians of Mecca and Medina and of the pilgrim routes from Africa (GHA V ch.6, p.138). Egypt fought the Wahhabis of Arabia in 1813–19, just before its 1820–3 Sudan campaign (GHA VI ch.13, fig. 13.1, p.328). In the current war, representatives of the SAF and RSF signed the Declaration of Commitment to Protect the Civilians of Sudan in Jeddah on 11 May 2023, facilitated by Saudi Arabia and the United States (JEDDAH2023).

Culture

Most Sufi orders in the Funj Sudan came from the Hijaz. Taj al-Din al-Bahari came from Mecca in 1577, at a Sudanese merchant-pilgrim's invitation, and brought the Qadiriyya; Hammad ibn Muhammad al-Majdhub, who studied in the Hijaz, brought the Shadhiliyya (GHA V ch.7, pp.179–180). Teachers from the Hijaz also worked in the early Funj kingdom (p.179).

Economy

The 'Sudan Road' from Darfur and Sennar to Sawakin carried both trade and pilgrims to the Hijaz. By the early 19th century it was known as far as Futa Toro, as a shorter and cheaper pilgrim route than the desert crossing through Egypt (GHA V ch.7, p.181). Ottoman Egypt sent cash, grain and the Ka'ba cover to the Hijaz (ch.6, p.143); no figures for Sudanese trade with Arabia were read.
