Capitals / sites

Alexandria

Geopolitics

The Dodecaschoenus, about 120 km from Syene (Aswan) to Hiera Sycaminos, 'had belonged to the Ptolemies, but the rulers of Meroe ... had long since added it to their kingdom' (GHA II ch.7, p.208). Riad attributes Meroe's prosperity under Ergamenes and his successors 'essentially to its friendly relations with Egypt' (ch.6, p.203). Shared Egyptian–Nubian sanctuaries at Philae and Dakka confirm continuing relations (ch.10, p.287).

Culture

Diodorus says Ergamenes, 'steeped in Greek culture', defied the priests who could order a king's death and had some of them killed; GHA notes there is no corroboration for the priests' power over the king's life, and his identity among three Meroitic kings is uncertain (GHA II ch.10, p.287). His temple at Dakka is 'purely Egyptian in conception' (ch.6, p.203). At Musawwarat es-Sufra, hymns in good Ptolemaic Egyptian honour the Meroitic lion-god Apedemak (ch.10, p.287).

Economy

The Ptolemies set up 'bases for elephant-hunting all along the African coast as far as Somalia' and built ships to carry the animals, because they needed war elephants against the Seleucids (GHA II ch.6, p.185). Riad says the only lasting result was cultural: Hippalus' discovery of the monsoon pattern, under Ptolemy III, shortened the voyage to India (p.185).
