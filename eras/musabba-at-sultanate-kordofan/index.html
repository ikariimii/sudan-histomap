Geopolitics

Founded in Kordofan by Keira princes who had lost out in Darfur. In Nachtigal's account, Tansam, uncle of Sulayman Solonga, was driven east after losing the struggle for Dar Fia (Takana 2016, pp.5, 49). Its stated aims were to regain the Fur throne and to hold Kordofan as a base for that. Three sultans tried and failed to take the Fur throne. The most serious attempt was Hashim's (1772–1786). Sultan Tairab then marched on Kordofan, Hashim fled to the Funj, and the Fur ruled Kordofan for 35 years until the Turco-Egyptian victory at Bara in 1821 (Takana 2016, pp.25–26). Holt & Daly call Kordofan 'a debatable land between the rulers of Sennar and Darfur' (HD2011, intro p.8).

Culture

The Musaba'at were 'kinsmen of the Fur Keira' (Takana 2016, p.25). Al-Tunisi lists them among the Muslim sultans east of Jebel Marra who were subdued by Sulayman Solonga (Takana 2016, p.10). Takana says the customs of eastern Darfur (Dar Dali) were 'influenced more by those of the Sinnar and Musaba'at kingdoms than by those of the Fur sultanate' (p.131).

Economy

No source read describes the Musaba'at economy itself. Holt & Daly note that rivalry over Kordofan, together with the Shilluk on the White Nile, made the direct caravan route from Kubayh to Sennar via El Obeid unsafe, so caravans went north from El Obeid to Shendi (HD2011, intro p.8). Holt & Daly do not name the Musaba'at; the link is the period and the region only.
