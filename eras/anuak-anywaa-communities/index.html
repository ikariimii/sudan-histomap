Geopolitics

Evans-Pritchard classes the Anuak with the Shilluk-speaking (Luo) Nilotes, and says they occupy a continuous territory (1940, pp.3, 5). Seen from the Nuer side, the Pibor river separated the Lou Nuer from the Anuak, the Lou's use of the Geni and Pibor was disputed by the Anuak and Beir, and the Nuer had evacuated part of 'Anuakland', partly because of tsetse and stock losses (pp.62, 68, 112). Small pockets of Anuak on the Sobat had been 'conquered but not absorbed' by the Nuer (p.221). This is an outsider's (Nuer-focused) account. Evans-Pritchard's Anuak monograph (EP1940_ANUAK) was not read.

Economy

The Anuak are described as a people with 'few or no cattle', whom the Nuer held in contempt for it (Evans-Pritchard 1940, p.16). The Nuer obtained good dug-out canoes from the Anuak by exchange or theft (p.72), and possibly tobacco from the Anuak of the Baro and Gila rivers, though more likely by raiding (p.88). This is a Nuer-centred, 1930s account and does not describe Anuak production directly.
