# Kufra–RSF supply-route check (LSM_KUFRA2024 → UN Panel of Experts)

Checked 2026-10-20 by Sudan Geopolitical Analyst.

## Document
- **Symbol:** S/2024/65 (as printed on the document itself)
- **Date:** 15 January 2024 (Distr.: General). Letter dated 15 January 2024 from the Panel of Experts on the Sudan to the President of the Security Council, transmitting the Panel's final report under para. 2 of resolution 2676 (2023). The report went to the 1591 Committee on 22 December 2023 and the Committee considered it on 15 January 2024.
- **Panel:** Panel of Experts on the Sudan established pursuant to resolution 1591 (2005)
- **URL:** https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=S/2024/65&l=en&t=pdf
- **Local copy:** /workspace/sudan-histomap/db/refs/S_2024_65_EN.pdf (sha256 dcb5a13db5f31e82fab78eaa6cca51e485594cd6892c02fd1937bacffbcbd4cc). It is not in the site repo.

## Verbatim quotes
Summary (p. 2): "The RSF takeover of Darfur relied on three lines of support: the Arab allied communities; dynamic and complex financial networks; and new military supply lines running through Chad, Libya and South Sudan."

Para. 44 (pp. 15–16): "According to various sources, in particular among members of southern Libyan communities such as Tebu and Darfurian armed groups based in Libya, RSF used another supply route into Darfur, from southern Libya. RSF procured substantial quantities of fuel from there. Some elements of the Darfurian armed movements in Libya, who had facilities to buy fuel at cheaper rates thanks to their relations with the Libyan National Army (LNA), played a leading role in that smuggling. Tanker trucks belonging to the movements and escorted by some of their elements transported the fuel to RSF in North Darfur through the Libya-Chad-Sudan tri-border area, often through Am Djarass."

Para. 45 (p. 16): "RSF also purchased cars from Libya, in particular Landcruisers, which, such as fuel, were essential to their military tactics based on mobility and pace. For example, the Panel was aware of a case in September when RSF imported several dozen new cars from southern Libya. Cars were gathered in Sabba, then were brought in Darfur to Zuruk by drivers hired by RSF, through Kufra. ..."

Para. 46 (p. 16): "RSF was also able to procure weapons through Libya. For example, the Panel was aware that, during the first weeks of the conflict, RSF asked for the cooperation of a Darfurian armed movement based in Libya in transporting artillery items and ammunition obtained in Libya to Darfur, ... While the movement's field commanders were in favour of that deal, it did not happen because the head of the movement vetoed it."

Para. 47 (p. 16): "In the operations from Libya, RSF benefited from coordination with several LNA brigades based in southern Libya. These included Subul al-Salam, a predominantly Zuwaya, Salafist brigade based in Kufra and controlling the border with the Sudan, with whom some RSF forces were already cooperating for smuggling activities before the war. According to various Libyan sources, Subul al-Salam was instrumental in facilitating the replenishment of RSF with fuel, cars and ammunition. The 128 Brigade, which had a presence in southern localities such as Sabba and was the main Libyan partner of Darfurian armed movements, was also involved."

Para. 147 (p. 40): "Cross-border trade between Libya and Darfur stopped at the end of April 2023. It resumed with commercial trucks heading from Kufra to El Fasher in May. ... which fuelled smuggling of this key good [diesel] from Libya to Darfur, including for the resupply of Sudanese warring parties."

## Verdict: PARTLY SUPPORTED
- **Supported:** the RSF used a supply route from southern Libya for fuel and Land Cruisers (paras. 44–45). Coordination came from LNA brigades, including Kufra-based Subul al-Salam (para. 47). The report attributes this to "various sources" and "various Libyan sources", not to the Panel's own direct finding.
- **Overstated:** the draft says the route ran "via Kufra for fuel, Land Cruisers and weapons". In the report, Kufra is named as the transit point only for the September car convoy (para. 45). Fuel went through the Libya–Chad–Sudan tri-border area, "often through Am Djarass" (para. 44). The one weapons case described "did not happen" (para. 46). Ammunition appears only in the sourced claim about Subul al-Salam's role (para. 47).
- **Not checked:** later Panel reports (S/2025/… and after). They were not checked in this pass.

One car convoy passed through Kufra, and LNA-aligned brigades, including the Kufra-based Subul al-Salam, reportedly helped resupply the RSF with fuel, cars and ammunition (UNPOE_S2024_65, paras. 44–47)." ## Verbatim extracts from S/2025/239
Para. 62 (Sect. VI.B "Libya", p. ~19): "The security situation in cross-border areas remained fragile. Since May 2024, Darfurian armed groups continued to operate in southern Libya, as follows: ... • SLM-MM defectors from the early 2000s, primarily Zaghawa and commanded by Jarelnabi Abdul Kareem. This contingent included 200 to 300 fighters stationed around the borders between Chad and North Darfur. The group was engaged in providing security for oil smuggling operations and the transportation of used cars for resale on behalf of RSF ..." (The other listed groups are JEM factions near Sabha and Atrun, Harun Makhir's group near Ouzou, and Mansour Arbab's group near Jufrah.)

Para. 63: "In early September, forces under the command of Abdallah Banda and Mansour Arbab crossed the border from southern Libya into eastern Chad, using routes around Tina before proceeding to El Malha in North Darfur. The primary objective of this manoeuvre was to reinforce SAF-aligned joint forces in the El Fasher area ..."

Para. 71 (Sect. VIII.A "Libya", p. 24): "Oil smuggling across the Libyan-Sudanese border continued unabated, fuelling economic instability and further entrenching armed group control in the region. A single drum of smuggled oil (250 litres) in southern Libya was priced at $600, but nearly doubled in cost when reaching Darfur. RSF sought to dominate several key smuggling routes, including the following: • Owainat mountains – Hamrat Al Sheikh, North Kordofan, North Darfur • Kufra – El Malha – (North Darfur) • Kufra – Mellit (North Darfur) • Sabha – Zuruk (North Darfur)" (fn. 76: interviews with SLA/AW members, traders, diaspora and researchers, Aug.–Nov. 2024)

Para. 72 (p. 25): "In August, the Sudanese Sovereign Council discussed the closure of the border with Libya to disrupt RSF military logistics. However, the limited military capacity of SAF hindered the effective enforcement of that decision. Control of the Libyan-Sudanese border remained fragmented among various actors. Forces aligned with Khalifa Haftar managed the stretch from Sabha to Jufrah and monitored smuggling routes from Chad to Darfur. Meanwhile, the SAF-aligned joint force, predominantly Zaghawa fighters, controlled the Altroe to Bin Hansin border zone."

Para. 94 (Sect. XI, p. 29): "RSF expanded logistical supply lines through territories in Chad and Libya, as well as Nyala Airport ..."

Para. 98 (p. 30): "... By mid-2024, as SAF intensified operations around Zuruk, RSF established a new logistics command post at Bir Mirgui, North Darfur. Strategically positioned at the crossroads of Am Djarass, El Fasher and other transit routes, Bir Mirgui became the central hub for transferring supplies from Eastern Chad and Southern Libya to active frontlines."

Para. 99 (pp. 30–31): "... Additional RSF commanders, including Fadeil El Naji and Mohamed Bakhit Ajab al-Dor ("Doydoy"), both former Sudan Revolutionary Awakening Council (SRAC) members, facilitated logistics between Chad, southern Libya and Darfur."

Para. 100 (p. 31): "... Using Nyala airport could be a way for RSF to take delivery of supplies more quickly than by the longer land routes from southern Libya and eastern Chad. ..."

Para. 104 (p. 31): "On 19 November, a mobile unit commanded by Abdelmajed Hassan (GSLF/Janna faction) ambushed RSF vehicles that were transporting ammunition from southern Libya to Darfur. The ambush occurred at Jebel Eredi (North Darfur). ... The Panel requested information from the possible manufacturers of the ammunition and from the relevant authorities in two Member States in order to trace the origin of the military materiel ..."

Annex 5 (p. 37): "... key areas for supplies through which fuel, ammunition, and military equipment has been moving from eastern Chad and southern Libya to Sudan since the onset of the current conflict in Sudan."

**Not mentioned in S/2025/239:** Subul al-Salam, the 128 Brigade, and the Zuwaya (a full-text search returned 0 hits). The report also gives no specific "tri-border" passage. Am Djarass appears only in connection with the Chad airport (para. 95: "The Panel was unable to confirm transfers of military materiel from Am Djarass to Darfur at the time of writing").

## Verdict: CONFIRMS AND EXTENDS (Libya route); does not re-confirm the brigade roles
- **Confirms:** the southern Libya supply line continued into 2024, for fuel (para. 71) and ammunition, including one interdicted ammunition convoy (para. 104), with Bir Mirgui as the hub (para. 98).
- **Extends:** Kufra now appears explicitly as the start of two smuggling routes that the RSF "sought to dominate", Kufra–El Malha and Kufra–Mellit (para. 71). Haftar-aligned forces controlled the Sabha–Jufrah stretch (para. 72).
- **Does not repeat:** the 2024 attribution to Subul al-Salam and the 128 Brigade. That claim still rests on S/2024/65 para. 47 alone ("various Libyan sources"), so it stays single-source.
- **Still not established:** the origin of the ammunition (the trace was pending) and any state supplier through Libya.

## Suggested updated wording
EN: "UN Panel of Experts reports describe a southern Libya supply line for the RSF. In 2023, fuel and Land Cruisers came from southern Libya, and one car convoy passed through Kufra. Libyan sources said the Kufra-based Subul al-Salam brigade and the 128 Brigade helped resupply the RSF (UNPOE_S2024_65, paras. 44–47). In 2024, the RSF sought to control smuggling routes from Kufra to El Malha and Mellit, and from Sabha to Zuruk. Supplies from southern Libya and eastern Chad were routed through a hub at Bir Mirgui, and an RSF ammunition convoy from southern Libya was intercepted in North Darfur in November 2024 (UNPOE_S2025_239, paras. 71–72, 98, 104)."
